How confident should you be, given what you’ve seen?

Bayesian thinking is a simple rule: start with what you believed, look at the evidence, and update. The charts below show that happening live. Drag anything.

1 · The coin flip: beliefs update with evidence

Question: how often does this coin land heads? We can never know for sure, so we track how plausible each answer is.

Where the curves overlap, we can’t tell the variants apart. More visitors make the curves narrower and the overlap smaller. “Credible interval” = the range we’re 95% sure contains the true value. Lift is relative: +18% means B converts 18% more often than A.

3 · Does your starting belief matter?

Two people see the same data. One is a skeptic who expected a 5% conversion rate; the other is an optimist who expected 20%. The truth, which neither knows, is 10%.
